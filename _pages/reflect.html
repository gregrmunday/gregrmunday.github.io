A small model with explicit assumptions

Sunlight on a sphere

The reduced latitude–longitude grid has about 100 km spacing, with fewer cells towards each pole. Every mean and power balance uses each cell’s spherical area. Continents and vegetation are illustrative, not Earth observations.

Kepler’s equation determines the orbit, its changing speed, and solar distance. Incoming radiation varies as 1361/r² W/m²; axial tilt sets solar declination. Daily mean sunlight includes polar day and night. Perihelion is near 3 January; its solar longitude is held at 282.94°. Display lighting is illustrative; physics uses daily means.

Learned land retains the supplied C45 coefficients, normalisation and 16-point Gaussian quadrature. Eight-point daylight quadrature averages its black-sky kernels; white-sky kernels determine diffuse reflection. Snow, ice and physical C45 inputs are prescribed, with no automatic climate feedback.

Scattering, without clouds

The global dial scales a smooth, heterogeneous per-tile scattering field. Each cell divides sunlight into a direct beam and a scattered contribution. The scattered field spreads with a spherical heat kernel, a Gaussian approximation with the selected width.

Pairwise equal and opposite power transfers conserve total incoming radiation, wrap around longitude, and cross polar rows. Positive stable substeps prevent negative light. Scattering changes local direct/diffuse albedo and where the light arrives. It does not add heat diffusion, clouds, atmospheric absorption or backscatter to space.

Brushes apply a circular Gaussian falloff. Albedo edits prescribe reflectivity rather than changing heat capacity or vegetation. Inspect any tile to edit its albedo and scattering multiplier precisely.

Energy becomes temperature

CO₂ forcing is ΔF = 5.35 ln(C/280) W/m². Three paired anomaly models integrate Cₛ dΔT/dt = ΔASR + ΔF − λΔT, where λ = 5.35 ln(2)/ECS. ECS values are 2.5, 3 and 4°C per doubling, using the IPCC AR6 assessed likely range (17th–83rd percentile convention) and best estimate.

Ocean heat capacity is 2.1 × 10⁸ J/m²/K (roughly 50 m of water); land is 2 × 10⁷. Each model integrates daily with an exact linear temperature update. Temperature responds over time; it is not the instantaneous imbalance multiplied by sensitivity.

A simultaneous unchanged 280 ppm reference uses the Earth-like orbit and original surfaces/scattering. Its annual radiation is calibrated to a prescribed 14°C global mean baseline; seasonal temperatures evolve too. The main imbalance is the change relative to that control. Total imbalance and stored heat include its seasonal cycle. Surface and orbital experiments are never recalibrated away.

The shaded temperature envelope spans these sensitivity scenarios. It is not a 17th–83rd percentile forecast for this toy model. This is a single-layer educational EBM, without dynamic heat transport, ocean circulation, ice feedback, carbon cycle or a validated forecast.
