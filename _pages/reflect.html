Paint a surface experiment

Choose a surface brush in “04 Paint a response”, select Draw surface, and drag on the globe. Focus Amazon centres the forest for a cover experiment. Brushes have a circular Gaussian footprint: strongest at the centre and zero at the edge. Low/high vegetation brushes transfer existing cover into the chosen category, preserving total vegetation cover. Their leaf-area targets are illustrative presets: 2 m²/m² for low vegetation and 5 for high vegetation. Bare ground removes cover and leaf area; snow adds up to 150 mm water-equivalent depth. These brushes preserve soil moisture and temperature. Vegetation conversion on a tile without vegetation has no effect.

Each changed land tile recalculates the supplied learned coefficients. Physical brushes clear prescribed albedo overrides in their land footprint. Elevation brushes change the learned albedo elevation input by up to 100 m per stroke, bounded to 0–9000 m; they do not deform the rendered sphere or cast terrain shadows. Tile inputs lets you set all eleven learned albedo inputs independently: high/low cover, high/low leaf area, elevation, snow, top/deep soil moisture, top/deep soil temperature and near-surface air temperature. The temperature controls use kelvin and affect reflection only; they do not set the simulated climate temperature. Exploration bounds are not validated training ranges. These changes affect surface reflection only: tree removal does not release carbon or alter evapotranspiration, clouds or circulation in this model.

The original surface inputs, BRDF coefficients and seasonal reference remain unchanged. Sunlight forcing shows the global change in absorbed radiation immediately; FaIR integrates the subsequent temperature response while running. Restore tiles restores the original vegetation, elevation, snow, albedo scheme and scattering in the patch without resetting temperatures or history.

Sunlight on a sphere

The reduced latitude–longitude grid has about 100 km spacing, with fewer cells towards each pole. Every mean and power balance uses each cell’s spherical area. Land, vegetation cover, leaf area and elevation come from the pinned SpeedyWeatherAssets boundary maps. The mask and static fields are sampled at half-degree resolution; monthly soil moisture, temperature, snow and sea ice retain their original 96 × 48 source resolution. Small islands and coastal cells can require neighbouring valid source data.

Kepler’s equation determines the orbit, its changing speed, and solar distance. Incoming radiation varies as 1361/r² W/m²; axial tilt sets solar declination. Hourly sunlight uses each tile’s longitude, latitude and solar zenith angle, including polar day and night. Each step evaluates radiation at its hourly midpoint; Greenwich solar noon is fixed at 12 UTC and the equation of time is omitted. Perihelion is near 3 January; its solar longitude is held at 282.94°. The calendar starts in today’s season at 12 UTC. The globe keeps one decorative viewing turn per model year, while its day/night shading follows the physical subsolar point. Playback rates are targets; slower devices take longer without skipping model hours. Automatic outputs arrive every 24 model hours; each samples the same UTC time, so the displayed day/night boundary does not sweep through a day. All intervening daylight hours still affect radiation and temperature. Use +1 hour to inspect the diurnal cycle. Pausing, inspecting and editing refresh the current state immediately.

Learned land retains the supplied learned albedo coefficients, normalisation and 16-point Gaussian quadrature. Black-sky kernels use the current hourly solar zenith angle; white-sky kernels determine diffuse reflection. Startup samples 24 solar phases at each of 48 seasons, then adds an approximate periodic diurnal thermal response. The monthly fields are interpolated between the current and next calendar month at startup, following SpeedyWeather’s first-of-month convention, then held fixed unless explicitly edited. Snow mass is converted to water-equivalent depth. Soil moisture uses the two-layer swl1/swl2 initialisation convention. The asset bundle has no distinct deep-soil or 2 m temperature: its land-surface temperature supplies both soil-temperature inputs and the air-temperature proxy at startup. The tile editor can then change these three temperature inputs independently; they stay fixed as the simulated climate evolves. The tile inspector exposes these inputs and coastal fallbacks. The learned albedo equations and coefficients are unchanged; the prescribed albedo map is not used.

Open ocean uses Jin et al. (2011), ported from SpeedyWeather’s gm/albedo branch: Fresnel reflection, the roughness correction, clear-sky diffuse reflection, volume scattering and wind-driven foam. Cox–Munk roughness is diagnosed from the global 10 m wind dial. A bounded 4097-point wind-specific lookup interpolates direct ocean albedo at the hourly zenith angle; the sourced fractional sea-ice cover mixes with albedo 0.6. The final mixture is bounded to 0–1.

Scattering, without clouds

The global dial scales a smooth, heterogeneous per-tile scattering field. Each cell divides sunlight into a direct beam and a scattered contribution. The scattered field spreads with a spherical heat kernel, a Gaussian approximation with the selected width.

Pairwise equal and opposite power transfers conserve total incoming radiation, wrap around longitude, and cross polar rows. Positive stable substeps prevent negative light. Scattering changes local direct/diffuse albedo and where the light arrives. Gaussian redistribution does not add heat transport or interactive clouds. CACK sets the default atmospheric screening of land-albedo forcing. The diffuse illumination control changes the learned albedo direct/diffuse mixture and illustrative redistribution; it does not apply an additional attenuation to CACK.

Brushes apply a circular Gaussian falloff. Albedo edits prescribe reflectivity rather than changing heat capacity or vegetation. Inspect any tile to edit its albedo and scattering multiplier precisely.

Temperatures and heat stores continue from their current states after an edit or undo. The one-input explorer snapshots the selected land inputs and atmospheric state, resolves 24 solar phases, and varies one physical input uniformly over the selected area. Other inputs remain fixed. Its baseline retains the current heterogeneous learned albedo inputs. The equilibrium estimate assumes this single day's mean forcing persists indefinitely. No climate timestep is taken by this preview. The baseline uses learned and ignores manual fixed-albedo overrides; applying an input also clears those overrides. The preview leaves the climate paused. Clipping percentages report when the predicted reflection reaches physical bounds; training-domain limits are unknown.

CACK: land albedo to global energy balance

By default, edited land reflection is converted to top-of-atmosphere (TOA) forcing with Bright and O’Halloran’s CACK v1.0 monthly all-sky climatology (2001–2016). learned albedo still predicts land albedo; CACK describes how its change reaches space through the prescribed atmosphere. A positive albedo change gives negative absorbed-solar forcing: ΔF = −K Δα. The supplied 1° monthly mean is area-averaged to 2° and stored in a 389 kB compact derivative. Year-specific and uncertainty fields are omitted.

For hourly integration, each monthly kernel is normalised by reference monthly mean TOA insolation and weighted by the current hour’s illumination. This distribution is an approximation: CACK does not supply hourly cloud observations. The learned albedo change is evaluated under identical illumination, and its idealised two-stream contribution is replaced by the CACK contribution, avoiding double screening. The monthly atmosphere stays fixed even when orbit or diffuse illumination changes. Polar-night missing values become zero; artificial sunlight in those months has no observed kernel constraint. Ocean albedo and scattering-only changes retain the illustrative radiation budget.

Idealised budget and comparison

The optional two-stream atmosphere has transmission 1 − 0.25s, reflection 0.20s and absorption 0.05s in each direction, where s is the screening control. Repeated atmosphere/surface reflection is summed analytically, treating returned light as diffuse. The total solar energy absorbed by surface and atmosphere is the incident TOA flux minus atmospheric reflection and the surface-reflected light that escapes. The unchanged absolute budget and ocean/scattering changes use this scheme; it also supplies land-albedo forcing when the idealised comparison is selected. These optical coefficients are illustrative and spatially uniform, not a fitted CERES radiative kernel or calculated effective radiative forcing. With the idealised comparison selected, s = 0 recovers the unscreened limit. CACK is a perturbation kernel; it does not calibrate the absolute planetary energy budget. Gaussian scattering redistributes transmitted sunlight independently, conserving area-weighted power.

FaIR’s three-layer temperature response

The global temperature response uses the deterministic three-layer thermal equations from FaIR. Surface, upper-ocean and deep-ocean temperatures exchange heat. The forcing is 5.35 ln(C/280) plus the area-weighted change in solar absorption at the top of the atmosphere from your surface, scattering and orbital edits. Hourly matrix-exponential updates integrate these equations without importing Python or the full FaIR package.

Three deterministic thermal parameter sets are selected jointly from published FaIR calibration 1.4.1, with approximately 2.5, 3 and 4°C ECS under this app’s logarithmic CO₂ forcing. Heat capacities, exchange coefficients, feedback and deep-ocean efficacy are retained together. The app uses only these thermal subsets, not the complete calibrated FaIR carbon cycle, forcing histories or posterior ensemble. The shaded range spans sensitivity scenarios, not a probabilistic 17th–83rd percentile transient forecast.

Playback starts automatically at the dated NOAA CO₂ estimate shown beside the dial. All three thermal layers begin in equilibrium with that concentration; the reference seasonal and diurnal cycles are initialised approximately and periodically. This is an idealised present-CO₂ equilibrium, not a historical spin-up or an observed present-day temperature. The absolute baseline is prescribed.

The unchanged 280 ppm reference retains the original Earth-like orbit, surfaces, wind and scattering. Its prescribed baseline averages 14°C. The expanded climate context shows imbalance relative to that reference. The headline temperature and forcing isolate the edited surface from its matched control. Total imbalance and integrated heat also include seasonal storage. Regional seasonal and warming patterns each have their area mean removed before adding the matching global FaIR response. A separate three-layer response supplies the global reference seasonal and diurnal variation, so fast local land temperatures cannot add extra global warming. Global temperature, net flux and integrated heat follow these consistent thermal states. The regional maps remain illustrative; local seasonal amplitudes are not calibrated to observations.

Surface inputs are prescribed until explicitly edited: no interactive clouds, circulation, ice feedback or carbon cycle. Edits preserve the ongoing temperatures and history. This lightweight model is intended for exploration and future temperature-goal gameplay.
